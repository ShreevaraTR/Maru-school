# Maru Instagram: first 3 posts

Three carousels (1080 × 1350) that launch Maru's Instagram. They use the same visual system as the website redesign.

- `export/`: ready-to-upload PNGs (`p1-01.png` … `p3-06.png`) plus `feed-preview.png`
- `slides.html`: editable source for every slide (same colors and fonts as `../styles.css`)
- `render.js`: re-exports the PNGs after edits (`node render.js`, needs Playwright)

## The strategy in one line

**Why → Learn → Who.**
- Post 1 gives people a reason to care about Korean.
- Post 2 gives them something useful to save.
- Post 3 puts a real person behind the school.

Together they set up the three content pillars the account can keep using: **Inspire** (why Korean), **Teach** (bite-size lessons) and **Trust** (Henry and the course).

**Posting order:** publish Post 1 → 2 → 3, for example Mon / Wed / Fri. The grid shows newest first, so it will read *Henry · 5 words · Journey* (see `export/feed-preview.png`). Pin all three while the account is new.

**Shared system:**
- Night-ink and hanji-paper backgrounds.
- Dancheong color band across the top.
- 마루 seal logo and slide counter on every slide.
- Hangul used as design, not decoration-for-decoration's-sake.
- The hanok-and-lanterns night scene from the website hero.
- All key content sits inside the 3:4 grid-crop safe area.

## Post 2: Educational · "Your first 5 Korean words" (7 slides)

**1. Objective:** A genuinely useful, saveable beginner lesson. It drives saves and shares, which are the signals that grow a new account.

**2. Visual concept**
- The cover is dark ink, with the five words stacked in outlined Hangul fading off the edge, and a "Save this for later" chip.
- Each word slide sits on hanji paper with a faint manuscript grid (원고지) behind a paper "flashcard".
- On the card, each Hangul syllable block has its romanization directly underneath. This echoes the website's interactive 한글 moment and quietly teaches that Korean is built from syllable blocks.
- A small red seal in the corner gives a one-word Korean label (인사 = greeting, 감사 = thanks…).
- Below the card: English meaning, plus a green TIP.

**3. Slide-by-slide copy**

| # | Korean | Romanization | Meaning | Tip |
|---|--------|--------------|---------|-----|
| 1 | Pill: KOREAN FOR TOTAL BEGINNERS · H1: **Your first 5 Korean words** · 🔖 Save this for later · Swipe | | | |
| 2 | 안녕하세요 | annyeonghaseyo (an·nyeong·ha·se·yo) | **Hello** (polite) | Your go-to greeting for almost anyone: shop staff, teachers, new people you meet. |
| 3 | 감사합니다 | gamsahamnida (gam·sa·ham·ni·da) | **Thank you** (formal) | It's spelled 합 but said "ham": ㅂ turns into ㅁ before ㄴ. Korean has sound rules like this, and they're learnable. |
| 4 | 네 | ne | **Yes** | You'll also hear 네 as a friendly "uh-huh, I'm listening" in conversation. |
| 5 | 아니요 | aniyo (a·ni·yo) | **No** (polite) | That final 요 (yo) is what makes it polite. Spot it on 안녕하세요 too? |
| 6 | 주세요 | juseyo (ju·se·yo) | **Please give me… / …, please** | Put it after what you want: 물 주세요 (mul juseyo) = "Water, please." |
